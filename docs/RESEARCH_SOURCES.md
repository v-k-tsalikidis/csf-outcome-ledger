## Research Findings

- CSF 2.0 is a taxonomy of high-level outcomes and does not prescribe a universal implementation.
- Organizational Profiles are scoped and may include practices, status, rating, target priority, goals, rationale, and evidence artifacts.
- NIST permits organizations to choose assessment scales, which makes the scale and its interpretation a methodology decision rather than an inherent CSF score.
- CSF Tiers characterize cybersecurity risk governance and management rigor; NIST states that they are not maturity levels.
- NIST does not certify or endorse CSF products or implementations.
- Existing tools already cover Core browsing, spreadsheets, assessment scoring, artifact tracking, AI-assisted document analysis, and dashboards.
- A defensible remaining niche is a narrow, metadata-only decision ledger with mapping rationale, human approval, version tracking, and freshness checks.
